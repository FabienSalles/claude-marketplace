# Dossier de compétences and contractor CV

Two long-form artifacts a French contractor needs beyond a two-page CV: the **dossier de
compétences** (sold through an intermediary to an end client) and the **contractor CV**
(read by a technical decision-maker). Read when the reader is an intermediary, or when the
artifact being produced is a dossier.

## 1. Why the dossier is a different document

| | CV | Dossier de compétences |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Get someone hired | Answer a call for bids, convince an end client on one specific mission |
| Nature | Career history | Commercial document |
| Reading order | Read at or after first contact | Seen by the end client **before** meeting the consultant |
| Stake | One step of a process | Triggers or kills the interview |

The dossier is not a longer CV. It is a sales artifact for a named mission.

## 2. Dossier structure and volume

Documented by the specialist tooling ecosystem for French dossiers. The percentages are
volume shares of the document.

| Block | Share | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Header | ~15% | Agency logo, business manager contact, first name plus initial, precise job title, dated availability |
| Commercial hook | ~10% | 4 to 6 lines, **rewritten for each mission**: sector, key technologies, one quantified result, the role |
| Technical skills | ~15% | Table by category, with level **and** years |
| Missions | ~50% | Context, 3 to 5 quantified achievements each, stack, dates |
| Education and certifications | ~10% | — |

**Most common error: inverting the ratio** — long skills section, short missions. The
missions block carries half the document; the skills table is a fifteen-percent index, not
the substance.

## 3. Format and anonymisation

- **PPTX** for working and personalising; **PDF** for the final send.
- A locked PDF complicates placement: the business manager needs to re-skin the document
  to the agency charter.
- Produce an **anonymisable variant** when the channel is an agency: first name plus
  initial, no contact details.

## 4. Contractor CV structure

Documented by the UK contracting ecosystem, transposable to the French market.

Order: **Profile → Key skills → Key projects/achievements → Contracts → Earlier career →
Qualifications.**

Lexical rules:

- Name the section **"Missions"**, **"Contracts"** or **"Positions"** — never "Employment
  history". The lexical choice signals whether the person understands the model.
- Where a personal company or umbrella structure is involved, write **"engaged to"** rather
  than "hired to", and mention the structure.

## 5. Twenty-plus engagements without eleven pages

Two documented approaches. Present both; they combine.

1. **Detail-and-condense.** Detail the last five to seven years. Condense everything before
   that into an "Earlier career" block of one-liners.
2. **Group.** Group micro-engagements rather than listing them one by one, or use a single
   umbrella entry under the person's own company with clients listed inside it by date.
   This shows continuity and prevents the profile reading as job-hopping.

Explicit warning from the sources: **listing every short engagement separately produces the
opposite of the intended effect — it reads as instability.**

## 6. Per-mission shape

Open each mission with **one or two bullets of client CONTEXT before any achievement**:

- sector
- client size
- purpose and scope of the role
- team structure
- what performance was measured on

Without that context a quantified achievement is uninterpretable. Then two to three
achievement bullets.

**Bullet density:** one line is thin, two lines is the right format, three only as a last
resort.

## 7. Renewals as a signal

State renewals explicitly, in the dates line: *"Jan 2024 to Dec 2024, extended twice"*.

On the buying side a renewal is read as proof that a client chose to keep the person. An
unbroken run of short engagements **with no renewal** is read the opposite way.

## 8. What the buyer rejects on

From the buy-side sources:

| Rejection trigger | What the buyer reads |
|---|---|
| Trading up skills — positioning on a competence not yet acquired | They will be paying to train the contractor |
| Non-achievements: "was trained on X", "obtained certification Y", with no client benefit | No proof of delivered value |
| A run of short engagements with no renewal | No client chose to keep this person |

Liberating counterpoint, also from the sources: **gaps between engagements are expected and
normal in contracting**, and a contractor CV does not have to account for the entire past.

## 9. Page-one economy

The buy-side sources describe page one being scanned in **ten seconds**. Budget it:

- a four-line profile
- about **ten skill bullets mirroring the client's requirements**
- roughly **four achievement bullets** proving similar engagements completed

Page two carries the recent, relevant missions.

## 10. French commercial fields

An intermediary filters on these **before reading anything technical**, and no
international CV template has a slot for them. Include them:

- daily rate
- availability date
- mobility (region, remote, hybrid)
- engagement duration and notice
- legal structure

## 11. The two-artifact strategy

Same referential, two compilations. **Never a compromise between them.**

- Cutting a dossier down to two pages destroys the document intermediaries actually read.
- Expanding a CV to eleven pages destroys the one a technical decision-maker reads.
